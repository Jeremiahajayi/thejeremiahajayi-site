Questions people ask

What should a B2B SaaS product launch checklist include?

Six stages: defining the launch (the GTM brief, tier, owner and measures), building the message and plan, enabling sales and customer success, launch-week checks, launch day itself, and the 30 days after, when most of the pipeline is created. Most checklists stop at launch day, which is why so many launches get attention but no sales conversations.

How far in advance should you plan a product launch?

Six to eight weeks for a major (Tier 1) launch, three to four weeks for a notable feature, and about a week for a smaller update. Partner launches and press need the most lead time, because other people’s approvals set the pace.

What is a launch tier?

A way of sizing a launch to the release. Tier 1 is a new product, a major feature, a partnership or a new market; Tier 2 is a notable feature; Tier 3 is an improvement. Tiering stops teams from overspending on small updates or underselling big ones.

Who should own a product launch?

One person, usually product marketing, accountable for the plan and the date. Product owns what ships; sales and customer success own using it with customers. The owner makes sure all three are working to one timeline.

How do you measure whether a launch worked?

Agree the measures before launch: sourced and influenced pipeline, demo requests, sales usage of launch assets and early adoption. Then report against them after 30 days. Press coverage and social reach are useful signals, but they aren’t the goal.
